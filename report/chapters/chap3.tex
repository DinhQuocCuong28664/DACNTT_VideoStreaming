\chapter{Requirements Analysis}
\label{chap:requirements}

This chapter identifies who uses the system and what each group needs, states the functional and
non-functional requirements that follow, and models them as use cases, each specified step by step.

\section{Stakeholders and User Requirements}
\label{sec:stakeholders}

The system serves three kinds of people: those who watch videos, those who upload them, and the
staff who moderate and run the platform. Table~\ref{tab:stakeholders} lists each group, what it
needs, and the requirements of Sections~\ref{sec:functional-requirements} and
\ref{sec:nonfunctional-requirements} that answer those needs.

The visitor's need to watch without an account is deliberate: requiring registration before any
content can be seen would deter adoption, so authentication is asked for only where an action needs
an identity. The registered user plays two parts, viewer and uploader, and the staff roles are kept
apart so that the person who reads the error rates is not the person whose decisions produce them
(Section~\ref{sec:roles-design}).

\section{Functional Requirements}
\label{sec:functional-requirements}

Table~\ref{tab:functional-requirements} lists the functional requirements of the platform. Each has
an identifier so that it can be traced through the use cases, the design and the evaluation.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Functional requirements of the system}
\label{tab:functional-requirements}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.08\textwidth} p{0.22\textwidth} X}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{REQUIREMENT} & \textbf{DESCRIPTION} \\
\midrule
FR-01 & Registration and login & Users register with a username, email address, and password. Authentication uses JSON Web Tokens carried in the Authorization header. A new account must prove that it can receive mail at its address before it may upload videos; signing in with Google counts as that proof. A new password, at registration, reset or change, is refused when it is a commonly used one, contains the user's own details or appears in a known breach. \\
FR-02 & Password recovery & Users request a reset link by email; the token is single-use and expires after fifteen minutes. A completed reset also confirms the address. \\
FR-03 & Direct upload to S3 & The client requests a pre-signed URL from the backend and transfers the file directly to Amazon S3 without routing the payload through the application server. A file above 512~MiB is transferred in parts of 32~MiB, so that a part that fails is retried without repeating the whole file. \\
FR-04 & Automatic transcoding & Once the upload completes, the system transcodes the source into HLS renditions without any manual trigger. \\
FR-05 & Adaptive playback & The player switches automatically among the renditions produced for the video, chosen from six rungs between 144p and 1080p according to the source resolution, as the available bandwidth changes. \\
FR-06 & Visibility control & Each video is public, unlisted, or private. A public video is listed in the catalogue; an unlisted one is reachable only by its direct link and appears in no listing; a private one is visible to its owner alone. The owner can change the setting after upload. \\
FR-07 & Channel management & Users view their own uploads, manage visibility, update video details, and delete a video together with all of its stored artefacts. \\
FR-08 & Search and filtering & Users search by title, description, or tags, and filter by category. \\
FR-09 & Engagement & Users express likes and dislikes and post comments; a comment may be removed by its author or by the video owner. \\
FR-10 & View counting & The system records a view only after playback has genuinely started, and does not count repeated views from the same viewer within a short window. \\
FR-11 & Related videos & The watch page suggests further videos drawn from the same category and the same uploader. A suggestion is subject to the same visibility rule as the video itself, so the list never reveals one the viewer could not open directly. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{REQUIREMENT} & \textbf{DESCRIPTION} \\

FR-12 & Automatic screening & Before a video is published, the system samples frames from it and classifies them; a video with clearly violating content is removed automatically, and one with doubtful content is held back for review. Screening that cannot be completed holds the video back and does not publish it. \\
FR-13 & Viewer reports & A signed-in viewer can report a video for a reason chosen from a short list, and has at most one open report per video. \\
FR-14 & Staff review & Operators approve or remove the held-back and reported videos, and every decision carries a reason from a closed list. \\
FR-15 & Appeals & The owner of a removed video can appeal once per decision, an operator resolves the appeal with a note, and a person other than the owner cannot learn that the video exists. \\
FR-16 & Accountability & Every decision is recorded together with what the automatic screening had concluded, so that the rates of false positives and false negatives can be estimated, the latter from a random audit sample, and an auditor can read them without being able to change a decision. \\
FR-17 & Role management & An administrator assigns the roles operator, auditor and administrator, cannot change their own role and cannot demote the last administrator; every change is recorded. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Requirement FR-03 shapes the architecture more than any other. Routing a multi-gigabyte upload
through the backend would consume server memory and bandwidth in proportion to the number of
concurrent uploads and make the API server the bottleneck of the platform. A time-limited pre-signed
URL lets the browser send the file directly to Amazon S3, so the backend handles only a small JSON
request whatever the size of the file.

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\label{sec:nonfunctional-requirements}

The non-functional requirements are listed in Table~\ref{tab:nonfunctional-requirements}.
Section~\ref{sec:nfr-evidence} sets each of them against the evidence of Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Non-functional requirements of the system}
\label{tab:nonfunctional-requirements}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.08\textwidth} p{0.20\textwidth} X}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{ATTRIBUTE} & \textbf{REQUIREMENT} \\
\midrule
NFR-01 & Latency & Time-to-First-Frame should remain low enough that playback feels immediate; the measurement methodology is defined in Section~\ref{sec:qoe-evaluation}. \\
NFR-02 & Scalability & The pipeline must absorb a burst of concurrent uploads without losing work, processing them as capacity permits rather than rejecting them. \\
NFR-03 & Reliability & A failed transcoding job must not silently disappear: it must be retried where retrying could succeed, and must raise an alert carrying the reason when it cannot. The retry and alerting mechanisms that satisfy this requirement on the deployed path are described in Section~\ref{sec:challenges}. \\
NFR-04 & Cost efficiency & Compute cost must accrue only during actual transcoding, with no charge for idle capacity. \\
NFR-05 & Security of content & Private videos must be inaccessible to unauthorised viewers even when the exact manifest URL is known. \\
NFR-06 & Security of the API & Authentication endpoints must resist brute-force attempts, and the origin of cross-origin requests must be restricted to an explicit allow-list. \\
NFR-07 & Reproducibility & The entire infrastructure must be reproducible from source control through a single command. \\
NFR-08 & Maintainability & Changes must pass automated tests and security scanning before reaching the main branch. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

NFR-05 goes beyond ordinary access control. Restricting an API response is straightforward, but a
video is not delivered by the API: a content delivery network delivers it as hundreds of segment
files. Access control that protects only the API therefore leaves the content itself unprotected,
which is the gap Section~\ref{sec:content-protection} closes.
