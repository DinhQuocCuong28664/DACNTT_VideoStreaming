\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This chapter compares the results with the objectives of Section~\ref{sec:objectives}, then states
the strengths and limitations of the delivered system and the work that would extend it.

One result goes beyond the five objectives. The platform screens every upload with Amazon
Rekognition and holds doubtful videos for staff, who work in a console with separate roles:
operators decide, auditors read the error rates, and administrators assign roles. Each decision is
stored beside the machine's own verdict, so the error rates can be estimated with confidence
intervals, and the owner of a removed video can appeal (Section~\ref{sec:moderation-design}).

\section{Strengths of the System}
\label{sec:strengths}

The application tier never handles video bytes. The backend issues pre-signed URLs, records
metadata and answers queries, while video flows directly between the browser, Amazon S3 and
CloudFront. The load on the API therefore does not depend on video size or upload concurrency, and
a failure in the processing tier leaves published videos playable.

The processing tier costs nothing while idle, because a transcoding container exists only while a
job runs. For the part of the bill that the architecture controls, Section~\ref{sec:cost-analysis}
estimates \$3.40 a month against \$133.35 for an always-on instance; with screening and delivery
added to both sides, the saving is about 77\%.

Additional capacity turns directly into lower latency. Chunk jobs are ordinary AWS Batch jobs on
the same compute environment, so a larger vCPU quota encodes more chunks at once, and no chunk came
near the two-hour limit of a job (Section~\ref{sec:chunked-evaluation}).

The performance claims are measured rather than assumed. Measuring time to first frame on the
deployed CDN showed that its price class served Vietnam from Europe, and measuring from other
regions showed the same gap for Brazil; the two corrections reduced the median latency by a factor
of 6.6 from Vietnam and 32 from Brazil (Section~\ref{sec:qoe-evaluation}). The automated suite
likewise caught a cookie signing defect before deployment (Section~\ref{sec:testing}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Transcoding.} The bitrate ladder has six fixed rungs whatever the content. Optimising the
ladder per title or per shot can save bandwidth; Netflix reported reductions of 28\% to 38\% at
equal perceptual quality \cite{netflix2018dynamic}. Extra capacity also helps a short video little:
a 25-minute video took 18.0 minutes at 8~vCPU and about 15 minutes at 16~vCPU, because planning,
audio encoding and finalisation take about three minutes whatever the capacity. Production has a
Fargate Spot quota of 8~vCPU, so four 25-minute videos submitted together finished after 73
minutes. Below five minutes a video keeps the single-container path, where fixed overhead
dominates: about 66 seconds per job for a three-second clip (Section~\ref{sec:drain-rate}).

\textbf{Moderation.} Screening samples one frame every five seconds, so content shown for less
than that can pass. Its thresholds of 60 and 90 per cent follow the provider's guidance and were
not calibrated on this platform's content. The 147 backend tests of moderation cover the
implementation, but the error rates have not been measured on real traffic, so this report makes
no claim about the accuracy of the screening. Screening is also the largest line of the monthly
bill, 22.50~USD under the stated workload.

\textbf{Accounts and access.} A new password is checked against common passwords, the user's own
details and a breach service \cite{nist80063b}, but existing passwords were not rechecked and the
breach lookup is skipped when the service does not answer within two seconds. Mail to one address
is capped at five per hour, but the count lives in process memory and registration has no
CAPTCHA. Signed Cookies stay valid for two hours, so making a video private does not revoke
cookies already issued, and the system has no digital rights management.

\textbf{Operation.} Rate limits and view deduplication are held in process memory, which is
correct only while the backend is one process. That process runs on one instance in one region and
is the only single point of failure of the system. The CloudFront distributions live in a second
AWS account, because the primary account was not permitted to create them, which splits the
infrastructure across two sets of credentials.

\textbf{Interface.} Tags can be set only at upload, and a thumbnail cannot be replaced. The
account stores no language preference, so the e-mails sent by a transcoding job, which has no
request to read a language from, are written in both languages.

The limits of each measurement, such as single runs, a synthetic four-hour source and probes
inside AWS regions, are stated beside the result in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} and are not
repeated here.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The first extension is a per-title bitrate ladder chosen with a perceptual quality metric such as
VMAF. Encoding a short probe of the source at several operating points and keeping those on the
convex hull of quality against bitrate would lower delivery bandwidth, and with it the delivery
cost, at the same perceived quality.

The second is less fixed overhead on the chunked path. Each chunk pays a container start-up of tens
of seconds. Encoding several chunks in one longer-lived worker, or raising the minimum chunk
length, trades parallelism for less overhead; the measurements of
Section~\ref{sec:chunked-evaluation} suggest that the best choice depends on the video length and
the quota. A faster x264 preset, checked with VMAF on difficult clips, could shorten every chunk.

The third is removing the single point of failure. A second backend instance behind a
health-checked address needs a load balancer and a shared store, such as Redis, for the rate
limits, view deduplication and mail count; the persistent state is already in MongoDB Atlas and
Amazon S3. A CAPTCHA on registration and password reset, and distributed tracing across the S3,
SQS, Lambda and Batch boundaries, belong to the same step.

The fourth is tuning the screening from evidence. The console already records the reason codes of
reversed removals and an unbiased audit sample; after a few hundred decisions, the thresholds and
the label policy can be adjusted and the effect measured again.

Two measurements should also be repeated: the 1~vCPU baseline, with more runs and with memory held
constant (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-evaluation}), and time to first frame from residential
connections rather than from AWS regions (Section~\ref{sec:multiregion-measurement}). Further
ahead, GPU-enabled serverless encoding \cite{gpuserverless2024} and a more efficient codec such as
HEVC or AV1 next to H.264 would raise throughput and reduce bandwidth.
