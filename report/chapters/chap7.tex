\section{Strengths of the System}
\label{sec:strengths}

The processing tier costs nothing while idle and scales with the work. A transcoding container
exists only while its job runs, so the month of Section~\ref{sec:cost-analysis} costs 31.87~USD
against 309.56~USD for an always-on server, and a burst of uploads is absorbed by the queue instead of
failing: in the burst of Section~\ref{sec:burst-evaluation}, no video failed once its file had
reached Amazon S3.

The work grows with the content, not with the path. Every measured video needed between 4.6 and
5.8~vCPU-seconds per second of source, from a 36-second clip to a four-hour video, so both the cost
and the capacity needed for a given catalogue can be predicted from its length. The range is an
estimate rather than a constant: the clip had three rungs instead of six, and screening was off for
the two largest runs. Three of the four
sources were test patterns, which are easy to encode, so content that is harder to compress would
sit at the top of this range or above it.

The output is honest to the player. Every segment of a rung starts with a key frame at the same
instant, and the declared bandwidth of every rung equals the measured peak of its segments
(Section~\ref{sec:bitrate-evaluation}). The player therefore switches without a gap and chooses a rung
it can sustain: on a link of 500~kbit/s it settled on the rung that fits and played without a stall.

The application tier never handles video bytes. The API issues grants, records metadata and answers
queries, while video moves directly between the browser, Amazon S3 and CloudFront. The load on the API
does not depend on the size of the files, and the processing containers hold no database credentials,
so one function owns every processing state and a fault in a container cannot corrupt a record.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Playback.} The player relies on Media Source Extensions, because every request for a
playlist or segment must carry the signed policy as query parameters and the native HLS player of a
browser cannot add them. A browser without Media Source Extensions, such as Safari on older iPhones,
therefore cannot play. Signed Cookies would avoid this, but browsers block third-party cookies inside
an embedded player, which the platform supports. AES-128 keeps the files useless without a grant, but
it is not digital rights management: a viewer who may play a video can also save it.

\textbf{Transcoding.} The ladder has six fixed rungs whatever the content. On a long video the audio
job sits on the critical path, 10.8~of the 38.3~minutes of the four-hour run, and the chunks are
bounded by the twelve slots that the capacity of the two compute environments allows. That run used a
synthetic test pattern, which is easier to encode than a recording. Every video also waits
19 to 35~seconds for Fargate tasks to start, about a quarter of the time of a short clip
(Section~\ref{sec:size-evaluation}).

\textbf{Load.} The free MongoDB cluster slows down above 100 operations per second. During the burst
of 100 uploads, the first requests of new API instances waited up to 8.8~seconds on it, longer than
the three retries of the client, so 8 uploads were refused at creation although the pipeline had
capacity to queue them. Start-up on an unthrottled connection met its target of three seconds at the median but not in
the slowest of five runs.

\textbf{Moderation.} Screening samples one frame every five seconds and at most 120 frames, so
content shown between two samples can pass. On a long video the gap widens: a four-hour video is
sampled once every two minutes. The sound is not screened at all, and the thresholds of 60 and 90~per cent follow the provider's guidance rather than a
calibration on this platform's content. The console measures the error rates from human decisions,
but too few decisions exist yet for a rate to mean anything, so this report makes no claim about the
accuracy of the screening.

\textbf{Operation.} Deployment is run by the operator rather than by the pipeline, so a change
reaches production only as often as someone runs the steps, and a step left out is noticed only by
the checks that follow it. The whole platform also runs in a single AWS region. CloudFront serves the
web application from the edge, but an outage of that region stops uploads, processing and playback
alike, because every playback grant and key comes from the API in that region.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The first extension is a bitrate ladder chosen per video. Encoding short probes of the source at
several operating points and keeping those that give the best quality for their bit rate, measured
with a perceptual metric such as Video Multi-Method Assessment Fusion (VMAF), lowers delivery bandwidth at the same perceived quality;
streaming services report savings of this kind from per-title and per-shot
encoding~\cite{netflix2015pertitle,netflix2018dynamic}.

The second is a shorter critical path for long videos. Encoding the audio in windows that overlap the
chunks, with the same packet handling at the boundaries that the chunks already use, would remove the
wait for the audio job, and a larger capacity grant would raise the number of chunk slots without any
change to the design.

The third is a fallback for browsers without Media Source Extensions. The API could return playlists
in which every address carries its own signature, which the native player of the browser can follow
without adding parameters, while other browsers keep the current path.

The fourth concerns load. A longer back-off with jitter on the client, or a client-side queue for
uploads, would let a refused upload wait instead of failing, and a paid database tier would remove
the limit of 100 operations per second.

The fifth is calibrating the screening from evidence. The console already records the reason codes of
reversed decisions and an unbiased audit sample; after a few hundred decisions, the thresholds and the
label policy can be adjusted and the effect measured again. Further ahead, a deployment workflow that
assumes a role in the account would make releases routine, and serverless encoding on graphics processing units
(GPU)~\cite{gpuserverless2024} and a codec such as High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC) or AV1 next to H.264 would raise throughput
and reduce bandwidth.
