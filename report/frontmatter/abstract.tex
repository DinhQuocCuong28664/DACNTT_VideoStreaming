\begin{center}
\textbf{\large Building a Video Sharing Platform with an Automated HLS Transcoding Pipeline on a
Serverless Container and Event-Driven Architecture}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5em}

A video sharing platform must turn every upload, whatever its format and resolution, into
HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) renditions that a browser can play at adaptive quality. Transcoding is a
bursty workload: a server kept running to wait for uploads is idle most of the time, yet the platform
must still absorb many uploads that arrive at once.

This project designs, builds and evaluates a complete video sharing platform whose transcoding runs on
serverless containers driven by events. The arrival of a file in Amazon S3 is queued by Amazon SQS and
starts one AWS Step Functions execution per upload, which runs FFmpeg as AWS Batch jobs on AWS Fargate,
Spot capacity first and On-Demand capacity as overflow, so no compute exists while nothing is
processed. FFmpeg decodes the source once and writes up to six renditions from 144p to 1080p, each
with a capped constant rate factor and key frames aligned across rungs; videos longer than five
minutes are cut into chunks that are encoded in parallel and stitched into ordinary playlists. The
segments are encrypted with AES-128 and served by Amazon CloudFront only to requests that carry a
policy signed for that video. Uploads are screened with Amazon Rekognition, and staff decisions are
recorded so that the error rates of the screening can be measured. Eleven Terraform modules describe
the infrastructure, and two GitHub Actions workflows lint, test and scan every change.

Measured on the production system, a four-hour video was ready 38.3~minutes after its upload, and of one
hundred uploads started at once 92 reached the ready state while the other 8 were refused at creation
with a status the client can retry. Once cached, the requests before the first frame took
435~ms at the 95th percentile. Processing three hundred five-minute videos a month is estimated at
31.87~USD, against 309.56~USD with an always-on server.
